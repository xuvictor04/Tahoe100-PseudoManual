# TP53

*tumor protein p53* — 17p13.1

> [!summary] In one line
> TP53 encodes p53, the stress-activated transcription factor that couples damage, oncogene and metabolic signals to arrest, senescence and apoptosis, and the most frequently disrupted gene in human cancer.

## What it is

p53 is a modular tetrameric transcription factor. Two N-terminal transactivation subdomains recruit coactivators and are the surface MDM2 covers; a proline-rich region follows; a central core domain, an immunoglobulin-like β-sandwich holding a loop-sheet-helix and a structural zinc, makes the base-specific contacts; a C-terminal tetramerisation domain and a basic, heavily modified tail complete it. The tetramer reads two decameric half-sites of the form RRRCWWGYYY, and the degeneracy tolerated there is why the target repertoire is large — several hundred genes with reasonable support, a core few dozen of which reproduce across systems. Output is partly direct induction ([[CDKN1A]], [[MDM2]], BBC3, BAX, GADD45A, SESN1) and partly indirect repression: p53 itself is a poor repressor, and most of the genes that fall do so through p21 and the DREAM complex rather than through p53 binding their promoters. See [[The p21-DREAM axis]] and [[The p53 pathway]].

Control is chiefly post-translational. In unstressed cells p53 is made steadily and destroyed steadily by MDM2; ATM and CHK2 phosphorylation of the transactivation domain after double-strand breaks, ARF sequestration of MDM2 under oncogenic signalling, and ribosomal-protein binding under nucleolar stress each break the loop and let p53 accumulate. The negative feedback gives pulsatile rather than graded dynamics in single cells.

The mutational pattern is diagnostic of a DNA-binding protein: roughly three-quarters of TP53 mutations are missense and most fall in the core domain, split between DNA-contact residues (R248, R273) and residues whose loss destabilises the fold (R175, G245, R249, R282). Because p53 works as a tetramer, many mutants are dominant-negative. Germline mutation causes Li-Fraumeni syndrome; somatic loss or MDM2 amplification affects most solid tumours.

## Why it appears in this work

TP53 is the axis of the study rather than one of its measured outputs. Lines are split by TP53 status, the induction arm of [[p53 program P]] is defined by what separates those groups under p53-activating compounds, and the gene-level directions that everything else is signed against come from this split — see [[Signing genes by p53 direction]] and [[p53 nullification in cancer cell lines]]. TP53's own transcript level is close to uninformative here, which is worth saying plainly: the biology of interest is p53 protein activity, inferred from targets, not TP53 mRNA.

## What is uncertain

Which p53 outputs actually matter is still open. Mice lacking the canonical arrest, apoptosis and senescence effectors retain substantial tumour suppression, and separation-of-function alleles show that acute damage responses and tumour suppression draw on different transcriptional programs — so inferring consequence from target induction is not safe. The gain-of-function question remains genuinely contested, with strong evidence for dominant-negative action and weaker, more allele- and context-specific evidence for neomorphic activity. For this study the sharpest limitation is the genotype label itself: "TP53 wild type" and "TP53 altered" are coarse bins that collapse missense, truncating, deleted and silenced alleles, and some nominally wild-type lines are functionally p53-deficient through MDM2 amplification or upstream loss.

> Multifunctional transcription factor that induces cell cycle arrest, DNA repair or apoptosis upon binding to its target DNA sequence (PubMed:11025664, PubMed:12524540, PubMed:12810724, PubMed:15186775, PubMed:15340061, PubMed:17317671, PubMed:17349958, PubMed:19556538, PubMed:20673990, PubMed:20959462, PubMed:22726440, PubMed:24051492, PubMed:24652652, PubMed:35618207, PubMed:36634798, PubMed:38653238, PubMed:9840937). Acts as a tumor suppressor in many tumor types; induces growth arrest or apoptosis depending on the physiological circumstances and cell type (PubMed:11025664, PubMed:12524540, PubMed:12810724, PubMed:15186775, PubMed:15340061, PubMed:17189187, PubMed:17317671, PubMed:17349958, PubMed:19556538, PubMed:20673990, PubMed:20959462, PubMed:22726440, PubMed:24051492, PubMed:24652652, PubMed:38653238, PubMed:9840937). Negatively regulates cell division by controlling expression of a set of genes required for this process (PubMed:11025664, PubMed:12524540, PubMed:12810724, PubMed:15186775, PubMed:15340061, PubMed:17317671, PubMed:17349958, PubMed:19556538, PubMed:20673990, PubMed:20959462, PubMed:22726440, PubMed:24051492, PubMed:24652652, PubMed:9840937). One of the activated genes is an inhibitor of cyclin-dependent kinases. Apoptosis induction seems to be mediated either by stimulation of BAX and FAS antigen expression, or by repression of Bcl-2 expression (PubMed:12524540, PubMed:17189187). Its pro-apoptotic activity is activated via its interaction with PPP1R13B/ASPP1 or TP53BP2/ASPP2 (PubMed:12524540). However, this activity is inhibited when the interaction with PPP1R13B/ASPP1 or TP53BP2/ASPP2 is displaced by PPP1R13L/iASPP (PubMed:12524540). In cooperation with mitochondrial PPIF is involved in activating oxidative stress-induced necrosis; the function is largely independent of transcription. Induces the transcription of long intergenic non-coding RNA p21 (lincRNA-p21) and lincRNA-Mkln1. LincRNA-p21 participates in TP53-dependent transcriptional repression leading to apoptosis and seems to have an effect on cell-cycle regulation. Implicated in Notch signaling cross-over. Prevents CDK7 kinase activity when associated to CAK complex in response to DNA damage, thus stopping cell cycle progression. Isoform 2 enhances the transactivation activity of isoform 1 from some but not all TP53-inducible promoters. Isoform 4 suppresses transactivation activity and impairs growth suppression mediated by isoform 1. Isoform 7 inhibits isoform 1-mediated apoptosis. Regulates the circadian clock by repressing CLOCK-BMAL1-mediated transcriptional activation of PER2 (PubMed:24051492)

**Summary**, from NCBI Gene [7157](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/7157):

> This gene encodes a tumor suppressor protein containing transcriptional activation, DNA binding, and oligomerization domains. The encoded protein responds to diverse cellular stresses to regulate expression of target genes, thereby inducing cell cycle arrest, apoptosis, senescence, DNA repair, or changes in metabolism. Mutations in this gene are associated with a variety of human cancers, including hereditary cancers such as Li-Fraumeni syndrome. Alternative splicing of this gene and the use of alternate promoters result in multiple transcript variants and isoforms. Additional isoforms have also been shown to result from the use of alternate translation initiation codons from identical transcript variants (PMIDs: 12032546, 20937277).
