# TP53I3

*tumor protein p53 inducible protein 3* — 2p23.3

> [!summary] In one line
> TP53I3 encodes PIG3, a p53-induced oxidoreductase-family protein tied to the reactive-oxygen arm of the p53 response, and the gene whose promoter microsatellite gave the field its first polymorphic p53 response element.

## What it is

PIG3 — p53-inducible gene 3 — was one of fourteen transcripts recovered in a transcriptome-wide search for genes turned on as p53 kills a colorectal cancer cell. Several of those genes encoded redox-active or mitochondrial proteins, which led to a model in which p53 does not only switch on apoptotic machinery but also raises reactive oxygen species that damage mitochondria and so commit the cell. PIG3 became the representative of that arm.

The protein belongs to the medium-chain dehydrogenase/reductase superfamily and is related to zeta-crystallin, an NADPH-dependent quinone oxidoreductase; that homology is why it is usually annotated as a putative quinone oxidoreductase. "Putative" is doing real work in that phrase. A crystal structure and enzyme assays do exist, and they report NADPH-dependent reductase activity on ortho-quinones — but far weaker than zeta-crystallin's, and on substrates chosen by analogy, so the physiological reaction remains uncharacterised.

Its regulation is unusual and better established than its catalysis. p53 activates the gene not through a canonical decameric response element but through a pentanucleotide microsatellite, (TGYCC)n, in the promoter. Despite only limited resemblance to the consensus, this repeat is both necessary and sufficient for p53-dependent activation, and because the repeat number varies between individuals it was the first p53 response element shown to be polymorphic — longer alleles giving stronger induction. Later work linked PIG3 to the early response to DNA damage: depleting it weakens H2AX phosphorylation and the recruitment of 53BP1 and the MRN complex to break sites, which makes it a participant in damage signalling rather than purely a downstream executioner.

## Why it appears in this work

PIG3 stands for p53's metabolic and redox output, which is a different category of effect from arrest and from apoptosis and is easy to overlook when the readout is a list of induced genes. It is in this vault as a curated effector rather than as a gene the data selected, so its presence is a statement about the pathway's known structure, not a result. For a reader of this study, its value is as a check: if the redox arm moves alongside [[p53 program P]], the response being measured looks like a full p53 program rather than only its cell-cycle component.

## What is uncertain

Almost everything mechanistic. That PIG3 raises reactive oxygen is not pure inference — a catalytically dead mutant fails to do it — but the demonstration used overexpressed protein and surrogate substrates, and the physiological reaction is still unknown, so the step from "has weak quinone reductase activity in vitro" to "generates ROS in a stressed cell" is not closed. The ROS-as-executioner model it supports came from strongly overexpressed p53 and has never been cleanly separated from the possibility that reactive oxygen is a consequence of commitment rather than a cause. The microsatellite-length association with cancer risk has been tested repeatedly in small cohorts with inconsistent results and should be treated as unresolved. Practically, PIG3 is only modestly expressed in most cultured lines, so any per-gene association computed for it in a single-cell dataset carries more measurement noise than the well-expressed effectors do.

## References

- Polyak et al., 1997, Nature — identified PIG3 among p53-induced genes and proposed the reactive-oxygen model of p53-induced apoptosis.
- Contente et al., 2002, Nature Genetics — showed a polymorphic promoter microsatellite mediates p53 induction of PIG3.
- Porté et al., 2009, Journal of Biological Chemistry — structure and enzymology of PIG3: weak NADPH-dependent ortho-quinone reductase activity, and reactive-oxygen accumulation that requires a catalytically intact protein.
- Lee et al., 2010, Oncogene — PIG3 contributes to the early cellular response to DNA damage.
- Fischer, 2017, Oncogene — census of p53 target genes, placing TP53I3 among the recurrently reported direct targets.

**Function**, from UniProt [Q53FA7](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q53FA7) (reviewed):

> NADPH:quinone reductase which expression is induced by p53/TP53 and is involved in both the DNA damage response through homologous recombination repair, and p53-mediated apoptosis (PubMed:19349281, PubMed:33782397). Catalyzes the NADPH-dependent reduction of quinones, exhibiting a low enzymatic activity with beta-naphthoquinones and a strong preference for the ortho-quinone isomer (1,2-beta-naphthoquinone) over the para isomer (1,4-beta-naphthoquinone). Also displays a low reductase activity for non-quinone compounds such as diamine and 2,6-dichloroindophenol (in vitro) (PubMed:19349281). The reduction of 1,2-naphthoquinone concurrently generates reactive oxygen species (ROS), which may trigger apoptosis downstream of p53/TP53 (PubMed:19349281)

**Summary**, from NCBI Gene [9540](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/9540):

> The protein encoded by this gene is similar to oxidoreductases, which are enzymes involved in cellular responses to oxidative stresses and irradiation. This gene is induced by the tumor suppressor p53 and is thought to be involved in p53-mediated cell death. It contains a p53 consensus binding site in its promoter region and a downstream pentanucleotide microsatellite sequence. P53 has been shown to transcriptionally activate this gene by interacting with the downstream pentanucleotide microsatellite sequence. The microsatellite is polymorphic, with a varying number of pentanucleotide repeats directly correlated with the extent of transcriptional activation by p53. It has been suggested that the microsatellite polymorphism may be associated with differential susceptibility to cancer. Alternatively spliced transcript variants encoding different isoforms have been found for this gene. [provided by RefSeq, May 2011]
