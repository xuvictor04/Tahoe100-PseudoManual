> [!summary] In one line
> TIGAR encodes a p53-inducible phosphatase that lowers fructose-2,6-bisphosphate, slowing glycolysis and diverting glucose into the pentose phosphate pathway — making it a p53 target that protects cells rather than killing them.

## What it is

The name is the mechanism as originally proposed: TP53-induced glycolysis and apoptosis regulator. The gene, formerly C12orf5, was found as a p53-inducible transcript whose product resembles the bisphosphatase half of the bifunctional 6-phosphofructo-2-kinase/fructose-2,6-bisphosphatase enzymes — but only that half: it has the histidine-phosphatase fold and catalytic histidine, and no kinase domain.

Fructose-2,6-bisphosphate is the allosteric activator of phosphofructokinase-1, the committed step of glycolysis; hydrolysing it inhibits PFK-1, backs glucose-6-phosphate up into the oxidative pentose phosphate pathway and raises NADPH and so reduced glutathione. Lower ROS follows, and resistance to apoptosis with it. This makes TIGAR the clearest example of p53's cytoprotective arm: the factor that induces [[BAX]] and [[PMAIP1]] also induces a gene that keeps stressed cells alive and reducing. A second activity has been described: under hypoxia a fraction of TIGAR relocalises to mitochondria and complexes with hexokinase 2, raising its activity in a manner dependent on HIF1α.

The mouse genetics are slightly awkward. Tigar-null mice develop normally, so the gene is dispensable for homeostasis; what they cannot do well is regenerate intestinal epithelium after damage, a defect rescued by ROS scavengers and nucleosides. In an intestinal adenoma model, TIGAR loss reduces tumour burden and extends survival, and TIGAR is elevated in human colon tumours. On the organismal evidence it supports rapid proliferation, malignant proliferation included.

## Why it appears in this work

TIGAR is a curated p53 effector in the metabolic and redox arm of [[p53 program P]], and it is the member that most usefully complicates the reading of the programme. Induction of TIGAR does not mean cells are dying; it means p53 has engaged its antioxidant, survival-permitting branch, in the same direction as [[SESN1]] and distinct from both the arrest axis ([[CDKN1A]], [[The p21-DREAM axis]]) and the apoptotic effectors ([[BBC3]], [[PMAIP1]]). For a reader of this study it is the standing reminder that "more p53 output" and "more cell death" are not the same quantity, and that a signature aggregating both arms averages over opposing consequences. See [[Signing genes by p53 direction]].

## What is uncertain

Whether fructose-2,6-bisphosphate hydrolysis is the activity that matters in cells is a live question. The measured catalytic efficiency on that substrate is modest, the structural work shows an active site considerably more open than the specificity-restricted pocket of the bifunctional PFK-2/FBPase-2 enzymes, and activity on 2,3-bisphosphoglycerate has also been reported. Few studies have separated phosphatase-dependent from phosphatase-independent effects with catalytically dead mutants, so much of what is credited to the enzymology could belong to protein interactions — the hexokinase 2 result points that way itself, and was characterised under hypoxia in particular systems.

Two further cautions. TIGAR has reported p53-independent inputs, so its induction under a compound is not evidence that p53 is engaged; it corroborates a p53 reading only alongside more specific members of the programme. And its directional meaning for cancer is inverted relative to its origin: a p53 target ought to be tumour-suppressive, but the knockout mice and human tumour expression both argue that TIGAR supports tumour growth, making it better described as a context-dependent survival factor that p53 happens to control. The step from transcript to metabolite is also not short: fructose-2,6-bisphosphate is under layered allosteric and hormonal control, and a modest change in TIGAR mRNA need not move it measurably.

## References

- Bensaad et al., 2006, Cell — identified TIGAR as a p53-inducible regulator of glycolysis and apoptosis acting through fructose-2,6-bisphosphate and the pentose phosphate pathway.
- Li & Jogl, 2009, Journal of Biological Chemistry — structural and biochemical characterisation of TIGAR, showing a histidine-phosphatase fold with a more open active site than PFK-2/FBPase-2.
- Cheung et al., 2012, PNAS — mitochondrial localisation of TIGAR under hypoxia stimulates hexokinase 2 and lowers ROS and cell death.
- Cheung et al., 2013, Developmental Cell — TIGAR is dispensable for normal development but required for efficient intestinal regeneration and for tumour growth in an intestinal adenoma model.
