> [!summary] In one line
> STK11 encodes LKB1, the master upstream kinase that activates AMPK and twelve related kinases, and the tumour suppressor whose germline loss causes Peutz-Jeghers syndrome.

## What it is

LKB1 is a serine/threonine kinase that does not work alone: it functions as an obligate heterotrimer with the pseudokinase STRADα and the scaffold protein MO25/CAB39, which together activate it and set its subcellular localisation. In that form it is largely constitutively active — the always-on upstream element, with the regulation sitting in its substrates.

Those substrates define its reach. LKB1 phosphorylates the activation-loop threonine of AMPKα, and the equivalent residue in twelve other AMPK-family kinases: the salt-inducible kinases, the MARK/PAR-1 polarity kinases, the BRSKs, the NUAKs and SNRK. The AMPK arm is the best known: as AMP rises relative to ATP, AMPK becomes sensitive to LKB1 phosphorylation, and its activation shuts down anabolic ATP consumption and restrains mTORC1 through TSC2 and raptor. Cells lacking LKB1 fail to activate AMPK under energy stress and lose the associated checkpoint — the core of LKB1's metabolic tumour-suppressor account. The MARK and SIK arms carry polarity and CRTC/HDAC-dependent transcriptional functions, substantial and often underweighted.

Clinically, germline loss-of-function in STK11 causes Peutz-Jeghers syndrome: hamartomatous gastrointestinal polyposis, mucocutaneous pigmentation and markedly elevated lifetime cancer risk. Somatically, STK11 is among the more frequently inactivated genes in lung adenocarcinoma, often co-mutant with KRAS, and is also hit in cervical and pancreatic cancers; the STK11/KRAS double-mutant subgroup is recognised as immunotherapy-resistant and immune-cold.

## Why it appears in this work

STK11 is here as an upstream and contextual gene, not a p53 target: it is one of the routes by which p53 output can be lowered in a cell line that is TP53-wild-type by sequence. The link runs through AMPK, which on energy stress engages a p53-dependent metabolic checkpoint; losing LKB1 removes that input. Several of this panel's nominally wild-type lines carry truncating STK11 alleles, which is exactly the problem described in [[p53 nullification in cancer cell lines]] — a wild-type-versus-altered split defined on TP53 does not make the wild-type group homogeneous. STK11 belongs with [[CDKN2A]], [[MDM4]], [[PPM1D]] and [[ATM]] in that annotation layer rather than with [[p53 program P]].

## What is uncertain

The attenuation argument is narrower than it first appears. The AMPK-to-p53 checkpoint was characterised under energy stress; most compounds here act through DNA damage, replication stress or MDM2 inhibition, and whether LKB1 loss blunts p53's response to those is not established. LKB1 loss plausibly lowers basal and metabolic-stress p53 tone; it has not been shown to damp the responses measured here.

Whether AMPK is even the relevant effector for LKB1's tumour suppression is contested: LKB1 has twelve other substrates, in several models the SIK branch accounts for more of the phenotype, and AMPK itself can support or restrain tumour growth by context. "LKB1 loss removes a tumour-suppressive AMPK signal" simplifies an unsettled question. The p53 step is also partly open: AMPK-dependent p53 serine-15 phosphorylation is reported, but its contribution relative to ATM and ATR at endogenous levels is not well quantified.

One measurement caveat: STK11 transcript level is an unreliable proxy for LKB1 function. Truncating alleles may or may not reduce mRNA through nonsense-mediated decay, and a kinase-impairing missense allele leaves the transcript intact, so an mRNA panel cannot distinguish an LKB1-null line from a functional one. Variants of uncertain significance in this panel make that concrete.
