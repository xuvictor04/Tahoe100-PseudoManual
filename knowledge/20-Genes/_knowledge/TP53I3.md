> [!summary] In one line
> TP53I3 encodes PIG3, a p53-induced oxidoreductase-family protein tied to the reactive-oxygen arm of the p53 response, and the gene whose promoter microsatellite gave the field its first polymorphic p53 response element.

## What it is

PIG3 — p53-inducible gene 3 — was one of thirteen transcripts recovered in a transcriptome-wide search for genes turned on as p53 kills a colorectal cancer cell. Several of those genes encoded redox-active or mitochondrial proteins, which led to a model in which p53 does not only switch on apoptotic machinery but also raises reactive oxygen species that damage mitochondria and so commit the cell. PIG3 became the representative of that arm.

The protein belongs to the medium-chain dehydrogenase/reductase superfamily and is related to zeta-crystallin, an NADPH-dependent quinone oxidoreductase; that homology is why it is usually annotated as a putative quinone oxidoreductase. "Putative" is doing real work in that phrase. Measured activity on candidate quinone substrates is low and the physiological substrate has not been identified, so the enzymology remains a homology inference rather than a characterised function.

Its regulation is unusual and better established than its catalysis. p53 activates the gene not through a canonical decameric response element but through a pentanucleotide microsatellite, (TGYCC)n, in the promoter. Despite only limited resemblance to the consensus, this repeat is both necessary and sufficient for p53-dependent activation, and because the repeat number varies between individuals it was the first p53 response element shown to be polymorphic — longer alleles giving stronger induction. Later work linked PIG3 to the early response to DNA damage, where it contributes in a reactive-oxygen-dependent way to damage signalling rather than acting purely as a downstream executioner.

## Why it appears in this work

PIG3 stands for p53's metabolic and redox output, which is a different category of effect from arrest and from apoptosis and is easy to overlook when the readout is a list of induced genes. It is in this vault as a curated effector rather than as a gene the data selected, so its presence is a statement about the pathway's known structure, not a result. For a reader of this study, its value is as a check: if the redox arm moves alongside [[p53 program P]], the response being measured looks like a full p53 program rather than only its cell-cycle component.

## What is uncertain

Almost everything mechanistic. The substrate and physiological reaction are unknown, so claims that PIG3 "generates ROS" are inferences from the family it belongs to and from knockdown phenotypes, not from measured chemistry. The ROS-as-executioner model it supports came from strongly overexpressed p53 and has never been cleanly separated from the possibility that reactive oxygen is a consequence of commitment rather than a cause. The microsatellite-length association with cancer risk has been tested repeatedly in small cohorts with inconsistent results and should be treated as unresolved. Practically, PIG3 is only modestly expressed in most cultured lines, so any per-gene association computed for it in a single-cell dataset carries more measurement noise than the well-expressed effectors do.
