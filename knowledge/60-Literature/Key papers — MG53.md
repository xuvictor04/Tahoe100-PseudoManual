# Key papers — MG53

The MG53 literature to know before reading anything in this vault about [[TRIM72]]. Entries are
author-year-journal with what the paper showed; where a claim is contested, that is said here rather than
left to be discovered later.

> [!warning] On citations in this vault
> Nothing here carries a PMID or DOI, deliberately. These are papers I am confident exist and showed what is
> stated; verify before citing one in writing of your own. If a paper you expect is missing, that may mean it
> was not confidently recalled rather than that it does not exist.

## The founding work: membrane repair

| Paper | What it showed |
|---|---|
| **Cai et al., 2009, *Nature Cell Biology*** | MG53 as the nucleating factor for acute membrane repair in striated muscle. `Trim72`-null mice develop progressive myopathy and are far more vulnerable to membrane injury. The paper that defined the protein's primary function. |
| **Cai et al., 2009, *Journal of Biological Chemistry*** | The trigger for MG53 assembly at a wound is **oxidation**, not calcium — entry of extracellular oxidant is what drives oligomerisation. This distinguishes MG53 from the calcium-triggered dysferlin pathway and is the mechanistic detail most often got wrong. |
| **Weisleder et al., 2012, *Science Translational Medicine*** | Recombinant MG53 protein delivered exogenously protects muscle in injury models — the origin of MG53 as a candidate protein therapeutic. |

Related: work on MG53's partners at the repair site — caveolin-3, dysferlin, PTRF/cavin-1 — establishes that
it works as part of a complex rather than alone. The cardioprotection literature in ischaemia-reperfusion is
large and consistent in direction.

## The metabolic role, and the argument about it

| Paper | What it showed |
|---|---|
| **Song et al., 2013, *Nature*** | MG53's RING domain ubiquitinates the insulin receptor and IRS1; sustained overexpression in mice produces insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome. The paper that made MG53 a metabolic gene. |

**This is genuinely contested.** Subsequent work disagrees about whether *circulating* MG53 causes insulin
resistance in humans or whether elevated blood MG53 is a marker of muscle damage that accompanies metabolic
disease. Groups have reached opposite conclusions, and the disagreement extends to whether extracellular
MG53 can act on the insulin receptor at all. Treat any single paper on circulating MG53 and human metabolic
disease as one side of an open argument, and do not cite the causal version as settled.

## Myogenesis

| Paper | What it showed |
|---|---|
| **Lee et al., 2013, *Journal of Biological Chemistry*** | MG53 restrains myoblast differentiation. Relevant here because it means MG53's expression is entangled with the differentiation programme that produces it — so a correlation between MG53 and muscle-programme genes is expected on both directions of causation. |

## What the literature does *not* contain, and why it matters here

- **Almost nothing about MG53 and p53.** There is no established transcriptional or protein-level link in
  either direction. That is the honest starting position for this study, and it is why the study was framed
  as a search rather than a confirmation.
- **Almost nothing about MG53 in cancer cell lines.** The protein is studied in muscle, heart, lung and
  kidney injury models. A panel of 50 non-muscle cancer lines is outside the range where its biology has
  been characterised. See [[Why MG53 is hard to measure here]].
- **Very little about TRIM72 transcriptional regulation** beyond the muscle differentiation factors. Which is
  consistent with what this study found: the factors that would drive it are absent from these cells. See
  [[Transcription factor activity inference]].

The gap between "MG53 is a well-studied protein" and "MG53 mRNA in cancer lines is uncharted" is the single
most useful thing to hold in mind when reading this vault's findings.
