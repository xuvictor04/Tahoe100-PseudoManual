# TP53

*tumor protein p53* — 17p13.1

> [!summary] In one line
> TP53 encodes p53, the stress-activated transcription factor that couples damage, oncogene and metabolic signals to arrest, senescence and apoptosis, and the most frequently disrupted gene in human cancer.

## What it is

p53 is a modular tetrameric transcription factor. Two N-terminal transactivation subdomains recruit coactivators and are the surface MDM2 covers; a proline-rich region follows; a central core domain, an immunoglobulin-like β-sandwich holding a loop-sheet-helix and a structural zinc, makes the base-specific contacts; a C-terminal tetramerisation domain and a basic, heavily modified tail complete it. The tetramer reads two decameric half-sites of the form RRRCWWGYYY, and the degeneracy tolerated there is why the target repertoire is large — several hundred genes with reasonable support, a core few dozen of which reproduce across systems. Output is partly direct induction ([[CDKN1A]], [[MDM2]], BBC3, BAX, GADD45A, SESN1) and partly indirect repression: p53 itself is a poor repressor, and most of the genes that fall do so through p21 and the DREAM complex rather than through p53 binding their promoters. See [[The p21-DREAM axis]] and [[The p53 pathway]].

Control is chiefly post-translational. In unstressed cells p53 is made steadily and destroyed steadily by MDM2; ATM and CHK2 phosphorylation of the transactivation domain after double-strand breaks, ARF sequestration of MDM2 under oncogenic signalling, and ribosomal-protein binding under nucleolar stress each break the loop and let p53 accumulate. The negative feedback gives pulsatile rather than graded dynamics in single cells.

The mutational pattern is diagnostic of a DNA-binding protein: roughly three-quarters of TP53 mutations are missense and most fall in the core domain, split between DNA-contact residues (R248, R273) and residues whose loss destabilises the fold (R175, G245, R249, R282). Because p53 works as a tetramer, many mutants are dominant-negative. Germline mutation causes Li-Fraumeni syndrome; somatic loss or MDM2 amplification affects most solid tumours.

## What is uncertain

Which p53 outputs actually matter is still open. Mice lacking the canonical arrest, apoptosis and senescence effectors retain substantial tumour suppression, and separation-of-function alleles show that acute damage responses and tumour suppression draw on different transcriptional programs — so inferring consequence from target induction is not safe. The gain-of-function question remains genuinely contested, with strong evidence for dominant-negative action and weaker, more allele- and context-specific evidence for neomorphic activity. For Tahoe-100M the sharpest limitation is the genotype label itself: "TP53 wild type" and "TP53 altered" are coarse bins that collapse missense, truncating, deleted and silenced alleles, and some nominally wild-type lines are functionally p53-deficient through MDM2 amplification or upstream loss.
